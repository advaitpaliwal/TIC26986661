# A Temperate 2.1 Earth-radius Planet Candidate on a 4.0-day Orbit around the M Dwarf TIC 26986661

Advait Paliwal
Independent researcher, Sterling Heights, MI 48314, USA
advaitpaliwal@gmail.com

## Abstract

I report a transit-like signal on TIC 26986661 (LP 371-15), an M dwarf at 67.4 pc. It appears in TESS full-frame-image photometry from five sectors spanning 2020–2023. A transit fit gives a period of 3.996227 ± 0.000016 d, a depth of 3971 ± 390 ppm and a duration of 1.44 h. This implies a radius of 2.06 ± 0.12 R⊕ and an equilibrium temperature of 513 ± 24 K. The star is not a TOI or CTOI, and the SPOC pipeline lists no threshold-crossing event for it. The signal is present in raw and corrected flux and in three independent pipelines, and odd and even depths are consistent. No Gaia source lies within 60″. Difference-image centroids fall within 9″ of the target, and every source within 120″ is disfavoured. TRICERATOPS with Gaia companion limits gives FPP = 0.0175 ± 0.0017, so the candidate awaits high-resolution imaging.

## Star and search

TIC 26986661 (Stassun et al. 2019) has T = 13.3 (G = 14.49), Teff = 3395 ± 157 K, R★ = 0.325 ± 0.010 R☉, M★ = 0.306 ± 0.020 M☉ and Gaia DR3 RUWE 1.09 (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2023). ExoFOP lists no imaging or spectroscopic follow-up. The star has no TESS 2-minute data. I searched TESS-SPOC (Caldwell et al. 2020) and QLP (Huang et al. 2020a, 2020b; Kunimoto et al. 2021) full-frame light curves of nearby cool dwarfs that lack 2-minute data, using Transit Least Squares (Hippke & Heller 2019) over 1–12 d. Before any false-positive analysis, each detection had to pass three gates: the dip appears in raw as well as corrected flux, the pipelines agree on its depth, and difference images place it on the target.

## Transit fit

I fitted native-cadence light curves from Sectors 21 (1800 s), 45, 46, 48 (600 s), 72 (200 s) and a 200 s TESS-SPOC light curve from a 2026 special pointing. I used batman (Kreidberg 2015) and emcee (Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013), integrating over each exposure, assuming a circular orbit, and applying a TIC stellar-density prior (12.5 ± 0.3 g cm⁻³) and quadratic limb darkening from Claret (2017). The fit gives P = 3.9962274 (+0.0000155/−0.0000167) d, T0 = 2460263.6065 (+0.0023/−0.0031) BJD_TDB, Rp/R★ = 0.0582 ± 0.0030, b = 0.20 (+0.16/−0.14) (95% upper limit 0.46), a depth of 3971 (+385/−390) ppm and T14 = 1.44 (+0.03/−0.06) h (Figure 1). This implies Rp = 2.06 ± 0.12 R⊕, a = 0.033 AU, an insolation of 11.5 ± 2.9 S⊕ and Teq = 513 ± 24 K (zero albedo, full redistribution). T14/T12 = 17.4 (+1.2/−1.8), and no posterior sample is grazing.

## Vetting

The combined depths are 3201 ± 281 ppm in TESS-SPOC, 2669 ± 363 ppm in QLP and 3098 ± 381 ppm in TARS photometry. Raw and corrected fluxes agree in every sector. Odd and even depths differ by 0.5σ (SPOC), 0.8σ (QLP) and 0.8σ (TARS), and the phase-0.5 depth is consistent with zero in all three. Injected transits are recovered in every sector. Same-camera, same-CCD comparison stars (134 folds) show no common dip at the transit times. Transit times fall near momentum dumps no more often than shifted ephemerides do. The 2026 pointing observed one predicted transit at 2913 ± 1249 ppm, consistent with the fitted depth.

No Gaia source lies within 60″. Free-centroid fits to difference images from TESScut (Brasseur et al. 2019) land 1.0–8.8″ from the target in each of the five sectors. Fitting the TESS PRF at all eight Gaia sources within 120″ disfavours each relative to the target by a summed Δχ² of at least 101.

## False-positive probability

I averaged TRICERATOPS (Giacalone & Dressing 2020; Giacalone et al. 2021) over ten runs of 10⁶ draws each, with companion limits from Gaia astrometry and imaging via MOLUSC (Wood et al. 2021). It gives FPP = 0.0175 ± 0.0017 (range 0.0149–0.0205) and NFPP = 0. Almost all of the FPP (0.016) comes from a planet orbiting an unresolved bound companion. Eclipsing-binary scenarios at twice the period sum to 0.0013. The single-star transit model cannot test an equal-mass grazing binary on its own, and the star has no high-resolution imaging, so I make no validation claim (cf. Collier et al. 2026).

## Summary

TIC 26986661.01 is a 2.1 R⊕ candidate with Teq ≈ 510 K around a nearby mid-M dwarf. Its deep (0.4%) transit makes it a strong target for high-resolution imaging, ground-based photometry and radial-velocity mass measurement. Code and data are at https://github.com/advaitpaliwal/TIC26986661.
